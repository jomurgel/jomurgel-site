Every morning I pull a tarot card to help me both set intention for the day. They're not telling you quantitative truths, but illuminating areas of your life to pay attention to. I largely credit [The Creative Tarot](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/The-Creative-Tarot/Jessa-Crispin/9781501120244) by Jessa Crispin for getting me into this routine. I hightly recommend it as both a lighthearted intro to tarot and a solid explanation of each of the standard cards.

In tarot, there's no such thing as good or bad unless you read the cards that way — change can be good or bad. While I could debate the merits of energy and resonance en infinitum, I think we can all agree that everyone has a gut feeling that can help make important (or unimportant) decisions. Tarot is a tool I use to tune into those gut feelings, maybe give some explanation to how I'm feeling or help me get out of my head on those days where my morning coffee just isn't hitting like it should.

This morning I pulled the Queen of Pentacles (Also known as the Queen of Coins). There are many ways to read this card[^attribution], but I prefer to look at it as one that tends to her garden, planting seeds knowing that one day the hard work and patience will bare fruit. At the same time this card talks to nurturing existing ideas, taking care of yourself, and tending to your garden. I'd read this card is a reminder to take care of myself—that I'm no good to anyone or anything unless I'm pouring love into myself.

So I move forward with a greater sense of purpose knowing that everything that I'm doing right now is setting up future success or laying groundwork for future projects. Serendipitously how [Project Acorn](/project-acorn) came into being.

Tarot is helpful for giving me purpose and direction and allows me to work more thoughtfully. What it doesn't do is dictate my actions or predict the future. It does what it has always done and gives me a moment to reflect before moving forward into my day.
