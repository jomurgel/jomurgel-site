---
title: "More personal, on purpose"
description: "AI made the faceless, finished version of everything free. So I'm doing the personal one on purpose."
date: "2026-06-26"
tags: ["reflections"]
---

The last few years I've. treated everything I put online as a deliverable. As content. Proof that I'm viable and that I exist. If we're not generating *content* in 2026, are we even doing anything? Hard to tell. Everything I tried to deliver I tried to make as clean, polished, and impersonal as possible. What saw the public was the "professional" version of a thing, sanded down until it lost all aspects of what makes me, me. Then AI got good enough to make exactly that, instantly, for free, forever.

So the thing that became more important and personal was the process now. This changed how I used my energy going forward. When a machine can product infinite content, the faceless part stops being the safe choice. The scant thing is me, the person — a specific person, with a specific taste, with specific opinions, and an actual reason for making a thing.

I'm not anti-AI, for the record. I lead automation and AI work in my day job, and I reach for it deliberately when it's the right tool[^tool] — it's genuinely good at the parts of engineering that are mostly typing, parsing, or scanning docs. What I won't hand off is the judgment, the taste, or the why. Though I still won't touch it for art. That line hasn't moved — like the em dash, a hill I'll die on — same hill that I'll die on regarding using an em-dash.

Now I'm doing the less efficient thing on purpose. Writing here again, in my own voice. Putting the sketch next to the final. Crediting the people I learn from instead of laundering their work and pretending.

Community doesn't come from the finished versions. It comes from people trudging along and learning, letting people see the process, the person, and being transparent about where AI is used, if at all.

I want people to see me, flaws and all. I think I'm interesting, flawed, creative, and unique. So: more personal, more often. I'm going to venture a guess that it's making a comeback.

[^tool]: And it is just that, a tool — multifaceted, sure, but a tool nonetheless.
